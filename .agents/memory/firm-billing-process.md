All new billing is entered as a flat amount. The user said: “Flat fees. even if it is hourly it is still adjusted in the system as a flat rate.”

**Why:** This replaces a manual worksheet, not an hourly timekeeping product.

**How to apply:** Do not make timers, hours, or hourly rates the main entry flow. Preserve older time records without silently converting or duplicating charges.

Owner and assignee allocations each use the gross client fee as their basis, not the balance after another person's share. The owner may retain a full or partial share, including an explicitly chosen fixed amount; the assignee receives a straight gross percentage. The remainder belongs to the firm.

**Why:** The user clarified that an individual's 40% share of a $1,000 gross fee is $400, including when work is assigned out. They need to see both people's allocations, not a single combined percentage.

**How to apply:** Keep allocation owner separate from the AppColl owner text and operational assignment. Show each person's amount and the firm remainder; reject combined payouts above gross. Do not silently replace saved payout terms when a task is reassigned. Count one gross charge, with personal net earnings reflecting the viewer's own allocation.

Administrators import reviewed, per-client fee schedules. Codes alone are not unique service identifiers; a fee agreement may reuse a code for different services.

**Why:** The supplied agreement has distinct services sharing a billing code and mixes fixed, hourly, approval-based and adjustment fees, despite the firm's flat-amount billing workflow.

**How to apply:** Preserve separate schedule entries, jurisdiction, descriptions and original rate classification. Fixed fees may populate gross amounts; hourly/approval entries require an explicitly entered flat amount, never an automatically assumed total. Courtesy-markdown entries must not become positive charges; until credit accounting is explicitly supported, apply an approved discount to the substantive fee's gross amount. Treat document upload as structured schedule import, not archival of the full agreement. Saving a task's billing draft alone does not create a charge. After pre-billing/completion, keep draft edits and the one linked charge consistent; invoiced/paid amounts and allocations remain locked.

Completed/filed work enters the billing worksheet once unless it has already been pre-billed. Active work may be explicitly pre-billed before completion, appearing in both workflow and billing. Invoice inclusion and payment receipt are distinct events. Allow independent fee entries, expenses, and reimbursements back to the firm.

**Why:** The user clarified that they sometimes bill before full completion and still need to track all prosecution handoffs.

**How to apply:** Never equate completion, billing approval, invoicing or collection. Pre-billed work stays active until completed, then leaves the active dashboard but retains the same billing record, a persistent asterisk, its pre-billing date, invoice date and actual completion date. Completion must not duplicate the fee, move the original billables to a new month, or erase invoices/receipts. Keep reimbursement recoveries separate from legal-fee earnings.

Exports should remain basic: a raw detail sheet comparable to the supplied template, plus monthly billable totals with quarter-end summaries in March, June, September and December.

**Why:** The user requested “something raw and basic like this” and “another sheet” for month and quarter summaries.

**How to apply:** Retain gross/net-share detail and clear date bases. Include prior-year source rows when their current-year invoice or receipt contributes to the selected year's summary.
